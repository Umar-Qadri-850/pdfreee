Turning a folder of images into a single PDF is a common need: expense receipts, photographed notes, a portfolio, or scanned forms. The trick is controlling page size, orientation and margins so the result looks intentional rather than thrown together.

## Before you convert

A few checks save a second pass:

- **Resolution matters.** A tiny screenshot blown up to A4 looks soft. For print, start with a large source image; for screen use, moderate resolution is fine.
- **Straighten first.** PDFs do not fix a crooked phone photo. Use your phone's editing tools to crop and rotate before converting.
- **Consistent orientation.** If some images are landscape and some portrait, decide whether you want each on its own matching page or all forced to one orientation.

## Converting step by step

1. Add your JPG or PNG images to the [JPG to PDF tool](/jpg-to-pdf).
2. Reorder them into the sequence you want.
3. Choose page size, orientation, and margins.
4. Create the PDF and download it.

Everything happens in your browser, so no images are uploaded.

## Choosing page settings

**Page size.** Match the destination: A4 for most of the world, Letter for North America. "Fit to image" style options let each page match its image proportions.

**Orientation.** Use *portrait* for documents and *landscape* for wide photos or slides. If you force an orientation that fights your images, you will get unwanted white space or shrinking.

**Margins.** Zero margin gives an edge-to-edge look; a small margin gives a printable border and stops borderless printers from clipping content. Printers rarely print to the very edge, so a 5–10 mm margin is a safe default.

## Working with many images

- **Order them deliberately.** Rename files `01.jpg`, `02.jpg`, … so the sequence is obvious, then add them in order.
- **Watch the size.** PDFs of full-resolution phone photos get large quickly. If the result is too heavy, convert to a lower resolution first or review the [compression guide](/blog/compress-pdf-without-ruining-text).

## When not to convert

If people need to view the images individually — a photo gallery, product shots — keep them as images. A PDF is best when the images form a sequence: pages of a document, a receipt trail, or a scanned chapter.

## Going the other direction

If you have a PDF and need images of its pages, use [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg), which renders each page at a resolution you choose.

## The short version

Pick a page size that matches where the file is going, match orientation to your images, add a small margin for printing, and order the images deliberately before you convert. The rest is one click.
